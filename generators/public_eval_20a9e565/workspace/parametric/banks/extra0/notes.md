## Inferred rule

The gray L corners bound an inclusive crop of a future object in a spatial
growth sequence. They are metadata, so their pixels are replaced by the inferred
object or background. A crop can show only part of the object and can skip one
or more unseen members. All five supplied labels, including both test gold
outputs, are implemented by the same input-derived algorithm.

Partition foreground into projection bands and infer the direction from rising
object mass. Align the leading edges while retaining perpendicular coordinates.
All retained coordinates constrain a global palette permutation; undoing its
phase makes the shapes additive. Four-connected components of each newly added
support form corresponding tracks. Infer each track's binary-geometry period
and per-period displacement separately from its color period. Continue those
tracks and the constant inter-object gap until the crop identifies one future
member. Apply that member's palette phase and crop.

There are no stored original grids, output lookups, case-ID branches or template
recognizers in the reference. The independent inference oracle was frozen before
the constructive sampler. Its broader anchor search is kept separate from the
production rule's declared leading-edge grammar.

The original target indices are 4, 6 and 6 for the training cases and 4 and 3 for
the test cases, zero-based. In particular, the ladder crop skips a member; the
wave test shows only two rows and a partial width of its future object. Geometry
and color periods differ in the layered example (2 versus 3), which is why a
single shared period is an inadequate model.

## Native extension and sampler

The canonical `whole` setting preserves the inferred original task. `new_shell`
returns only coordinates added at the selected target step. A previously present
cell is not new merely because the palette permutation changes its color.

Two demonstrations teach differently colored new components, inherited support,
palette cycling, a skipped step and a partial crop. Six demonstrations and four
query pairs are constructed before count prefixes; the complete query bank is
privately shuffled independently of query ordinal. Request limits are exact
integer seeds `0..2**63-1`, 2–6 demonstrations and 1–4 queries. Input streams are
identical across settings, while their labels differ.

Five direct-coordinate grammars vary stems/bars, ladders, layered lobes,
periodic waves and nested arches. Primitive dimensions, increments, palette
cycles, gaps, crop windows, translation, rotation and reflection vary. Expected
labels come from the constructed future coordinates, independently of the
reference's inference algorithm. Ordinary/native builders are self-contained
and byte-identical; complete inputs are compared. Query 103 always supplies a
substantive 30×30 case, alternating the layered and ladder stress grammar by
seed parity.

## Domain and limits

The reference requires one mass-monotone projection, leading-edge alignment,
at least three complete objects, a constant positive gap, additive normalized
support, a fully observed palette bijection and stable ordered component tracks.
Geometry periods must repeat with a constant displacement. Color continuation
chooses the least compatible period. Multicolor components have fixed geometry;
monochrome component shapes may repeat periodically. The crop belongs to one
future object along the sequence axis, although it may clip its perpendicular
extent and unshown support may extend outside the canvas.

These are explicit sequence assumptions. Finite source-bound tests and named
rivals do not establish unique continuation against arbitrary programs or
all-seed correctness. No unrelated shared runtime, raw parent data, catalog,
approval, allocation policy or training gate was changed.
