# Decisions for 88b8e6aaa49d

## D1 — Replace, rather than preserve, the texture control surface

**Prior choice.** Version 1.1.1 advertised six controls: dimensions, palette size, texture, background density, and noise overlay. The texture helpers coupled observable properties, degenerate texture overrides bypassed ordinary construction, unknown keys were accepted, and the baseline override stage rejected or failed tens of thousands of combinations.

**New choice.** Expose only `grid_h`, `grid_w`, and `palette_size`. Each is an exact scalar, all combinations are supported, and `observe_overrides` recovers the realized value from pixels.

**Alternatives rejected.** I did not add compatibility declarations around old texture aliases because those names did not denote stable, independently controllable semantics. I also did not keep caller-opt-in symmetric/single-cell degenerates: they hide the target transformation and are poor training examples.

**Evidence.** Baseline and final override receipts are under `checks/before/` and `checks/after/`; final task tests exercise every axis in every band, all legal bounds, unknown/retired keys, invalid types, and deterministic replay.

## D2 — Bind the selected authority without changing it

**Prior choice.** No `RULE_HASH` was declared.

**New choice.** Pin `RULE_HASH = "d4b6baaa86e7"`. Production outputs remain generated by the selected canonical Racket rule; the Python mirror is used only for tests and semantic scoring.

**Alternative rejected.** I did not rewrite the canonical program to the syntactic alias `(rotate-180 g)` merely to satisfy a checker. That would mutate authority to work around a shared-tool parsing limitation.

**Evidence.** `rule_smoke`, `racket_lint`, and `authority_sync` are green in `checks/after/result.json`; no Racket or canonical path appears in the return patch.

## D3 — Use a five-profile deterministic curriculum

**Choice.** `LESSON_SCHEDULE_PERIOD = 5`, ordered as the three canonical 3×3 palette sizes, one rectangular generalization, and one larger-square generalization. `EXAMPLES_NEEDED = 3`; ordinary materialization uses three demonstrations and one query.

**Rationale.** The canonical examples are all 3×3 and use 4, 5, and 6 colors. A schedule that only sampled arbitrary larger textures missed that support in the baseline floor check. The two shape profiles add transfer without swamping the canonical manifold.

**Alternatives rejected.** Pure random profile selection could omit a canonical palette family in a short lesson. A three-profile canonical-only schedule would overfit square 3×3 geometry. More than five profiles would dilute short-window guarantees without adding a distinct semantic contrast.

**Failure modes and limits.** Fixed ordering can leak profile position to a model, although the target operation is identical in every profile. The production and rotated lesson checks show no declared-rival ambiguity, but do not exclude all conceivable order-dependent learners. The fixed-seed exhaustive schedule census proves coverage only for seed 0 and the declared period.

**Evidence.** `checks/lesson-census.json` and `checks/schedule-census.json`.

## D4 — Reject every non-target D4 equivalence at input time

**Choice.** An accepted input must differ from its 180° output and that output must differ from identity, horizontal/vertical flips, 90°/270° rotations, and both diagonal reflections.

**Rationale.** Correct labels alone are not useful if a simpler competing transform gives the same answers. This is cheap to test for a whole-grid transform and directly protects lesson identifiability.

**Alternatives rejected.** I did not rely on a frequency target (“most examples are asymmetric”), because even one ambiguous three-demo lesson can train the wrong shortcut. I did not declare `rot180` as a rival: it is the selected rule, and a counterexample to it would be mislabeled data.

**Evidence.** All 41 focused cases pass; four witness groups fire 600/600 in the final semantic report; the 300-lesson census has zero declared-rival survivors.

## D5 — Generate arbitrary cell arrangements, then validate

**Choice.** For exact dimensions and palette size, place every palette color at least once, fill remaining cells from the same palette, shuffle deterministically, and reject candidates that violate the semantic contract. Cap retries at 512.

**Rationale.** This is simpler and less shortcut-prone than maintaining a catalogue of shapes whose symmetries must each be reasoned about. It also makes every exact axis combination naturally constructible.

**Limit.** The distribution is intentionally broad and texture-agnostic; it does not mimic a named visual texture family. Across the battery, 600-draw semantic census, and 1,200-input lesson census there were no construction failures, but the retry cap is still a finite guard rather than a proof for all seeds.
