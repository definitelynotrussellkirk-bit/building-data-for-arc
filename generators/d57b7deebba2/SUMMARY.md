A 7–10 by 10–12 zero-background grid contains two or three colored endpoint
pairs. Each pair lies on one slope −1 anti-diagonal, has span length 2–5, and
has an empty interior. Different spans use different anti-diagonals and do not
overlap. A color may be reused on multiple anti-diagonals.

The rule groups nonzero cells by **both** color and anti-diagonal (`r+c`) and
fills every group between its minimum and maximum row. Thus a repeated color
can independently fill more than one anti-diagonal span.

The three-example curriculum schedules a repeated-color case every third
sample so the canonical-fitting shortcut “each color is one global pair” is
explicitly refuted in every consecutive lesson.
